# Competitor-analysis source notes

This directory records public facts about bot-programming competitions, official results, and statements published by organizers or participating teams. It does not estimate which techniques will transfer to Florent Code League and does not supply independent conclusions about the techniques described.

Research pass: 29 July 2026.

## Reading guide

- [Similar competitions](competitions.md) records how each organizer describes its event.
- [Lineage and name distinction](lineage.md) records the publicly stated Florent–Cambridge–MIT relationship and distinguishes Battlecode from BattleBots.
- [Cambridge Battlecode 2026](cambridge-battlecode-2026.md) separates the official Grand Finals result from the public ladder and records statements about finalists.
- [Published team accounts](published-team-accounts.md) summarizes only what named teams or team members reported.
- [MIT Battlecode](mit-battlecode.md) records official winners and selected participant postmortems.
- [Other recorded winners](other-recorded-winners.md) records organizer-published results from other bot competitions.
- [Sources](sources.md) is the link register.

## Evidence conventions

- **Official result** means a result displayed by the competition organizer.
- **Organizer statement** means a claim made by an organizer but not independently derived here.
- **Team account** means a claim made by a team or one of its members.
- Ladder rank and tournament placement are recorded separately.
- “No artifact located” means that this research pass did not locate one. It does not mean that none exists.

The catalogue uses broad inclusion: submitted programs compete in a game or simulated environment without live move-by-move control. Inclusion is not a claim that two games have identical rules or that a strategy transfers between them.
