# Team leaderboard — full round-robin

Every bot against every other bot. Each pairing is 15 maps × both side assignments = 30 games, mirrored so
Team A's first-mover advantage (~58–60%, G27) cancels exactly. The engine is deterministic given RNG-free
bots, so these are exhaustive results, not samples.

Generated by `tools/roundrobin.py`. Re-run after any change. **These are KNOWN-map results — the 15
published maps. See the unseen-map section below, which measures something different and more important.**

## Readings

**1. `vanguard` is not beating one bot — it beats the entire field.** 30–0 over luc1, 29–1 over lockin,
29–1 over starter_fixed, 28–2 over jonbot. This is a **general capability gap, not a rock-paper-scissors
matchup**, which is the important distinction: a matchup quirk could be ignored, a general capability
cannot.

**2. We are the closest anyone gets to it.** Every other bot manages 0–2 wins in 30 against vanguard; we
get 8. Whatever it has, we are least far from it.

**3. The gap is throughput, and it is arithmetic, not tuning.** Replay-level diagnosis of 14 games: they
field ~4.9 turrets per game, we field ~1.3. Our *tempo* is now equal (first gunner median round 13 vs their
14). One harvester delivers 2.5 Ti/round; a Gunner spends 2 ammo/shot and fires every round; so **one feeder
saturates at ~1.25 shots/round ≈ 12.5 damage/round**, and a 500 HP Core needs ~40 rounds of sustained fire
from a single battery. They pay that ceiling several times over; we pay it once. More guns per feeder does
*not* help — they split the same 2.5 Ti/round. The lever is more **feeders**.

**4. Their barrier ring is the second general capability.** Our shots: 102 landed on their Core, 154
absorbed short of it — 40% hit rate. Theirs against us: 1068 landed, 148 absorbed — 88%. Our Core has no
equivalent ring.

**5. `luc1` and `lockin` remain statistically tied** at 65 each, head-to-head 14–16 — a two-game margin,
inside the ±5 noise floor of G/M01. The anti-Lucas counter-tuning in `lockin` still has no measurable
effect.

## Unseen maps — the number that actually predicts ladder strength

Known-map results measure memorisation as much as strength. `tools/unseen.py` plays the same mirrored
sweeps on generated maps no bot has seen (`maps/generated/`, from Jon's corpus).

AutistimusPrime, 12 maps × 2 sides per opponent: starter_fixed 17–7, luc1 24–0, lockin 24–0,
jonbot 7–17, vanguard 5–19 — **77/120, with 59 core kills**.

Two things this exposes that the known table hides:

- Before the runtime siege planner, AutistimusPrime scored **zero core kills on every unseen map** — the
  attack silently disabled itself when the map was unrecognised. The known-map record was memorisation.
- `luc1` and `lockin` collapse to 0–24 against us on unseen maps, far worse than their known-map form,
  which suggests they are considerably more map-dependent than they look here.

**Standing rule: no change counts as a gain unless it holds on unseen maps too.** Run
`python tools/evaluate.py <bot>` — it reports both columns and labels the known one as memorisation.

## What this says about picking a trunk

- **`vanguard` is the strongest bot in the repo by a wide margin** and is the obvious trunk candidate. It
  also uses no map data at all, so its numbers are directly comparable to anyone's.
- `luc1` and `lockin` are no longer the leaders. Any trunk decision made on the previous table is wrong.
- AutistimusPrime's contribution is not just its second place: the ground-truth register (51 verified
  facts, 8 documentation errors found), the arena and unseen-map harness, the crash/validator gate, and the
  verified CPU-clean profile are things no other bot has, and they are what let this table exist.

## Caveats

- All measured on this six-bot pool. Ladder opponents will differ, and counter-tuning aimed at a teammate
  does not transfer.
- Under enforced per-turn CPU limits on Linux (G45), AutistimusPrime is completely clean; `lockin` is −3
  and `luc1` −2, at or inside the noise floor. Nothing reorders. Not yet confirmed on Graviton3/ARM.
